# OCODE Closest Viable Path to Vision Law v1.0

**Status:** OCODE_CANONICAL_GOVERNING_LAW  
**Scope:** Every OCODE roadmap, backlog, build, refactor, integration, experiment, platform choice, governance addition, and promotion decision  
**Failure mode addressed:** OCODE can spend repeated cycles improving infrastructure, governance, architecture, or local components while failing to prove or advance the central product vision.

## 1. Canonical law

> **OCODE must continuously select and execute the closest viable path to validated progress toward its governing vision. “Closest” means the fastest, most efficient, sufficiently safe, evidence-producing route that materially proves or advances the vision for the actual user and environment—not merely the route with the fewest steps, the quickest code output, or the most convenient local task.**

OCODE must re-evaluate the path whenever new evidence, capability, infrastructure, risk, cost, or opportunity changes the route ranking.

## 2. Governing vision

OCODE exists to become a universal, governed wrapper that can:

1. accept human intent, specifications, or existing code;
2. understand the user, environment, constraints, and desired outcome;
3. activate only the minimum sufficient laws and controls;
4. produce a platform-neutral semantic specification;
5. compare feasible implementation routes;
6. select and invoke suitable language, platform, model, and execution adapters;
7. generate, modify, translate, test, and explain code;
8. distinguish generation, compilation, execution, deployment, and real operation;
9. preserve ownership, portability, evidence, recovery, and handoff;
10. allow another compatible AI system or qualified human to continue the work.

A build is vision-proximate to the extent that it proves or enables this complete capability for a real use case.

## 3. Definitions

### 3.1 Validated vision progress

A measurable reduction in uncertainty or increase in demonstrated capability directly related to the governing vision. Examples include:

- a natural-language request converted into a platform-neutral contract;
- a real adapter producing working target code;
- tests proving required behavior;
- a user successfully directing, reviewing, correcting, and continuing the result;
- bidirectional code-to-specification reconstruction;
- a transferable continuation package;
- evidence that one implementation route is more suitable than another.

Documentation, architecture, schemas, or infrastructure count only when they directly enable a near-term proof and have an identified consumer.

### 3.2 Closest viable path

The route with the highest expected **validated vision progress rate** after accounting for:

- direct contribution to the vision;
- evidence and learning gained;
- probability of successful completion;
- user and dreamer fit;
- time to meaningful proof;
- cost and cognitive burden;
- complexity and dependency count;
- safety, legality, privacy, and truth requirements;
- reversibility and recovery;
- ownership, portability, and maintainability;
- lock-in and opportunity cost;
- reuse across future modules and adapters.

### 3.3 Enabling work

Infrastructure, governance, tooling, refactoring, or research that removes a verified barrier to a defined near-term vision proof.

### 3.4 Vision drift

Work that is internally coherent or useful but does not materially reduce a current barrier, produce external evidence, or move the system closer to demonstrating the governing vision.

## 4. Vision Progress Rate

OCODE must compare candidate paths using a qualitative or quantitative model equivalent to:

```text
Validated Vision Progress Rate
=
(Expected vision contribution
 × evidence gain
 × probability of success
 × user/environment fit
 × reuse value)
÷
(time to proof
 + cost
 + complexity
 + dependency burden
 + governance burden
 + lock-in
 + risk exposure)
```

The formula is a decision aid, not false precision. Assumptions, confidence, and unknowns must remain visible.

## 5. Mandatory path-selection process

Before locking a material next build, OCODE must produce a compact **Vision Path Selection Receipt** containing:

1. governing vision anchor;
2. current demonstrated state;
3. current weakest barrier to the vision;
4. candidate paths considered;
5. expected vision contribution of each path;
6. time and cost to evidence;
7. user and environment fit;
8. risks, dependencies, ownership, and maintenance effects;
9. selected path and reasons;
10. rejected or deferred paths and reasons;
11. smallest complete proof to deliver;
12. stop condition and maximum permitted commitment;
13. reassessment trigger;
14. evidence location.

At least two plausible paths must be compared for a material decision unless only one route is technically or legally viable. Trivial, reversible changes may use a compact single-route receipt.

## 6. Enabling-work admission test

An infrastructure, governance, synchronization, refactoring, or tooling build may precede a vision-facing proof only when all of the following are true:

1. it removes a named current barrier;
2. the next consuming build is identified;
3. the capability will be exercised in a bounded near-term proof;
4. a simpler workaround is materially worse or unavailable;
5. the work is time-boxed and has a stop condition;
6. its cost is proportionate to the evidence expected;
7. ownership and maintenance are acceptable.

If these conditions are not met, the work must be deferred, simplified, or reclassified as optional infrastructure.

## 7. Consecutive-detour limit

> **OCODE may not complete more than two consecutive enabling or governance builds without producing a vision-facing proof, user-visible capability, real adapter execution, or external evidence result.**

Reaching this limit triggers mandatory path re-ranking and scope reduction under the Anti-Paralysis and One-Move laws. High-stakes safety remediation is exempt only while the named hazard remains active and the exception is recorded.

## 8. Fastest does not mean reckless

This law cannot be used to bypass:

- truth and evidence boundaries;
- safety or qualified-authority requirements;
- informed human control and consent;
- legal, regulatory, privacy, or security obligations;
- ownership, credential, recovery, and portability requirements;
- essential maintainability and handoff controls.

A shortcut that creates unacceptable hidden risk, irreversible lock-in, false claims, or owner helplessness is not the closest viable path because it increases the expected distance to sustainable vision realization.

## 9. Build-ranking rules

When two candidate builds are both feasible, prefer the build that:

1. proves more of the end-to-end OCODE cycle;
2. reaches real user interaction sooner;
3. resolves the current weakest barrier;
4. generates stronger decision-changing evidence;
5. remains reversible and portable;
6. creates reusable adapters or semantic contracts;
7. requires fewer unsupported dependencies;
8. reduces future maintenance and handoff burden;
9. preserves the user’s understanding and control;
10. prevents additional speculative infrastructure from becoming necessary.

## 10. Promotion rule

A build may be described as the “next closest path” only when its Vision Path Selection Receipt shows that it outranks the plausible alternatives under the current evidence.

A completed build must report:

- vision progress actually achieved;
- evidence produced;
- assumptions disproved or confirmed;
- new barrier revealed;
- deviation from expected time, cost, or capability;
- whether the next path ranking changed.

## 11. Relationship to the Law Guardian

The OCODE Law Guardian must protect this law from both under-governance and over-governance:

- it activates the minimum controls necessary for the selected path;
- it merges duplicate path and feasibility checks;
- it identifies when governance burden is delaying the vision proof;
- it does not remove constitutional protections to improve speed;
- it recommends suspension or deferral of rules and builds that do not affect the current barrier;
- it produces one compiled path-selection control rather than multiple repetitive questionnaires.

## 12. Acceptance tests

1. A governance-plumbing build is deprioritized when a bounded end-to-end wrapper proof can be completed sooner.
2. A fast route that hides ownership, safety, or runtime limitations is rejected as non-viable.
3. A candidate path addressing the current weakest barrier outranks a locally convenient but indirect feature.
4. Two consecutive enabling builds trigger a required vision-facing proof or explicit recorded exception.
5. Material next-build decisions include alternatives, evidence expectations, stop conditions, and reassessment triggers.
6. New evidence causes path rankings to be recalculated rather than preserving an obsolete roadmap.
7. A small reversible proof is preferred over a large speculative platform build when both test the same assumption.
8. The selected path can be explained clearly to the user without exposing them to the entire internal law library.
